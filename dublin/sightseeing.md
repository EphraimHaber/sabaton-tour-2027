## 1. Whiskey

**If they only do one:** WH-02 Teeling, Distillery Select tasting. It is a working distillery (you see the stills running), the tasting has four whiskeys including a single pot still and a peated malt, and it is a 20-minute walk from Guinness and St Patrick's Cathedral. **If they do two:** add WH-01 Jameson Bow St with the Cask Draw - no distilling happens there, but it is the famous name, the cask-strength pour straight from the barrel is the best single sip on any Dublin tour, and it sits in Smithfield next to the Cobblestone (BP-02) and Collins Barracks (HI-03). Skip the Irish Whiskey Museum unless it rains and they are already at Trinity; skip Dublin Liberties (tours closed). For a bar instead of a tour: WH-07 Palace Bar or WH-08 Dingle Whiskey Bar, both central.

## 4. History and military interest

Ireland was neutral in WWII, so there is no major WWII site in Dublin. The military story here is the 1916 Rising, the War of Independence and Civil War, and the roughly 49,400 Irish dead of WWI. Collins Barracks is the one place that covers Irish soldiers across all of it.

### HI-01 - Kilmainham Gaol
- **Why:** The best history visit in Dublin. The 1916 leaders were held and shot here; the guided tour ends in the execution yard. Sabaton-grade storytelling.
- **Area:** Kilmainham, Dublin 8 (west)
- **Time:** 1 hour guided tour; allow 90 min
- **Price pp:** NIS 29 (EUR 8)
- **Booking:** **Essential, and only on the official site** - guided tour only, sells out; no third party is authorised to sell tickets
- **Hours:** Open all year except 24-27 Dec. Visitor centre 09:30-17:15, museum 10:30-17:15
- **Fits:** Tue 6, Wed 7 morning
- **Official:** https://www.kilmainhamgaolmuseum.ie/
- **Source:** https://www.kilmainhamgaolmuseum.ie/plan-a-visit/
- **Note:** The page does not say how far ahead tickets are released; start checking in early March 2027. No wheelie bags on the tour (small lockers only).

### HI-02 - GPO Museum (Witness History)
- **Why:** Inside the General Post Office, the rebel headquarters in 1916. Immersive account of the week-long battle in the building where it happened.
- **Area:** O'Connell Street
- **Time:** About 1-1.5 hours (duration not stated officially)
- **Price pp:** NIS 54 (EUR 15) online; NIS 61 (EUR 17) walk-in
- **Booking:** Online is cheaper
- **Hours:** Mon-Sat 10:00-17:00, last admission 16:00; closed Sundays and public holidays
- **Fits:** Tue 6, Wed 7, Thu 8 morning (late-flight options)
- **Official:** https://www.gpomuseum.ie/
- **Source:** https://www.gpomuseum.ie/

### HI-03 - Collins Barracks: National Museum (Soldiers & Chiefs)
- **Why:** Free museum in a huge 18th-century barracks; "Soldiers & Chiefs" covers Irish soldiers at home and abroad from 1550 on, including WWI and WWII. The closest match to Sabaton's subject matter.
- **Area:** Benburb Street, Smithfield / Stoneybatter (Luas Red Line, Museum stop)
- **Time:** 1.5-2 hours
- **Price pp:** NIS 0 (free)
- **Booking:** No
- **Hours:** Tue-Sat 10:00-17:00; Sun and Mon 13:00-17:00
- **Fits:** Tue 6, Wed 7
- **Official:** https://www.museum.ie/en-IE/Museums/Decorative-Arts-History
- **Source:** https://www.museum.ie/Decorative-Arts-History/Contact-Us (hours and free admission, via search snippet)
- **Note:** The museum site blocked automated access, so the current status of the Soldiers & Chiefs galleries was not re-verified today. Pairs with Jameson Bow St (10 min walk).

### HI-04 - Glasnevin Cemetery (Irish History Tour + O'Connell Tower)
- **Why:** Ireland's national cemetery: Michael Collins, de Valera, the 1916 dead. Guides are excellent; the tower climb (198 steps) gives a city view.
- **Area:** Glasnevin (north, bus from centre)
- **Time:** About 1.5 hours tour; half a day with travel
- **Price pp:** NIS 61 (EUR 17) Irish History Tour; NIS 39 (EUR 11) O'Connell Tower climb; NIS 46 (EUR 13) audio-visual guide + exhibition
- **Booking:** Recommended
- **Hours:** Tower access daily from 10:00 and from 13:00. Tour times not shown on the official ticket page (third-party guides say 11:30 and 14:30 daily - unconfirmed)
- **Fits:** Tue 6; Thu 8 morning for 17:20/17:30 departures
- **Official:** https://dctrust.ie/experience-glasnevin/Tickets.html
- **Source:** https://dctrust.ie/experience-glasnevin/Tickets.html and https://dctrust.ie/experience-glasnevin/tours-and-highlights/oconnell-tower.html

### HI-05 - 1916 Rebellion Walking Tour

- **Area:** Starts at The International Bar, 23 Wicklow Street

## 5. Active, outdoors and day trips

**Is the Cliffs of Moher worth it?** Not on the 2-night trip - there is no free day. On the 3-night trip it is possible only on Tue 6 Apr, costs the whole day (06:55-19:45, about 7 hours of it on a coach) and comes straight after a 22:15-23:05 landing. It would replace every Dublin activity except gig day. Recommendation: skip it; do the Howth cliff walk for sea cliffs in a half day at NIS 16 return. Pick the Cliffs only if all three would rather see the west coast than Dublin.

### OUT-01 - Howth Cliff Path Loop
- **Why:** Real sea cliffs, a lighthouse view and a fishing harbour, 30 minutes from the centre by train. Free, and the best half-day for three fit people.
- **Area:** Howth (north Dublin Bay), start and finish at Howth DART station
- **Time:** 2 hours walking (7.8 km, 130 m ascent, moderate); 4-4.5 hours door to door with lunch
- **Price pp:** NIS 0 for the walk; DART about NIS 8 (EUR 2.30) each way on a Leap card - the 2027 fare as reported; it is EUR 2.00 today
- **Booking:** No
- **Hours:** Daylight; DART from Connolly about every 15-20 min, about 30-35 min (third-party figures)
- **Fits:** Tue 6; Thu 8 morning for 17:20/17:30 departures only
- **Official:** https://www.discoverireland.ie/dublin/howth-cliff-path-loop
- **Source:** https://www.discoverireland.ie/dublin/howth-cliff-path-loop (walk); https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsidewest/articles/2026/09/10/fury-over-15-fare-hike/ (fare rise)

## 9. Where to stay, by vibe

**Docklands (next to 3Arena)** is the practical choice for a 2-night trip: a 2-15 minute walk home after the show, the airport coach (Dublin Express 784) stops outside the arena, and the Luas Red Line runs straight to the centre in 15-20 minutes - but the area is modern offices and goes quiet at night, so every evening out means a tram ride. **Temple Bar / city centre** puts the group in the middle of the pubs, whiskey bars and late food, walkable to almost everything in this file, at the cost of street noise, tourist-priced pints and a tram or 25-30 minute walk to the arena. **Smithfield / Stoneybatter** is the pick for this group if they stay 3 nights: it is on the same Luas Red Line as the arena (no change), Jameson Bow St, the Cobblestone, Collins Barracks, Underdog and The Black Sheep are all within 10 minutes on foot, Fibber Magees is 15 minutes away, and it feels like a neighbourhood rather than a tourist strip. For the 05:55 and 07:50 Thursday departures (DUB-03, DUB-09, DUB-11), Docklands or anywhere on the north side shortens the pre-dawn taxi.
